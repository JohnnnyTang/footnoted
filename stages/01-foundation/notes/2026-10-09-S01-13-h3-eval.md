## Measurements

**Method.** Release build, `android-x64`, on the emulator AVD `fn_api36` (`sdk_phone64_x86_64`, Android 16 / API 36, 4 vCPU, 2.5 GB RAM), **shared with the S01-11 and S01-12 apps, which were running concurrently**. Timings are the median of 3 runs inside one launch unless marked as a single run; the table gives the cross-launch spread. Corridors:

- **Walk:** 5.000 km, 32 vertices, a seeded right-angle street-grid walk in central Paris (48.86°N) with a 100 m buffer.
- **Drive:** 1,001 km, 178 vertices (one every ~5.6 km), eastwards from Bordeaux, meandering 44.8 ± 1.5°N, with a 500 m buffer. Ideal buffered area without overlap: 1,001.8 km².

**Grids.**

- **Quadkey:** supercover rasterise in Mercator cell space, then a disk dilation into run-length spans, with the radius taken per row from that row's latitude. Two radius rules: **`ceil`** (the handoff rule: r = ⌈b/w⌉, mask dx²+dy² ≤ r²) and **`exact`** (mask dx²+dy² ≤ (b/w)²).
- **H3:** one capsule polygon per line segment (16 chords per cap), then `polygonToCells` (centre containment) on each capsule, then the sorted union.

**Boundary error:** 20,000 seeded points spread uniformly in a ±2b band around the line, each with its exact distance to the polyline. *missed* is P(not covered | d ≤ b) × b, and *over* is P(covered | b < d ≤ 2b) × b. Both are equivalent metres per side.

Walk (one z10 tile): z20 ceil 260 vertices (65 rects), z21 480, z20→z16 16; H3 r11 outline 296 (1–6 ms), r12 outline 780 (87–119 ms).

**The structural difference.** Quadkey cells nest exactly inside map tiles. A tile query is a range query on (x, y), rollups are exact "any child covered" sets, and rectangles need no clipping. H3 cells do not align with tiles, so a z10 view of H3 coverage needs one of three things: millions of hexagons; an outline dissolve (seconds to minutes on this corridor, and it must be redone on every coverage change); or compacted parents, whose hexagons do **not** exactly cover their children (aperture 7), so the fog edge would shift between zooms. In every case the hexagons crossing a tile edge must also be clipped by polygon clipping.

**Blockiness at z16–z18.** Both levels are visibly blocky (16–128 dp steps); z21 halves the step but does not remove it. Blocky is the decided v1 look (handoff "Fog rendering"); soft edges are the later raster-mask polish, and that is where edge smoothness should come from.

**Do 100 m buffers make the extra resolution pointless? Yes.**

- With the `exact` rule, z21 improves the walk's edge by **1.3 m per side** (7.8 → 6.5 m).
- That is below phone GPS accuracy (typically 5–15 m) and far below the arbitrariness of a 100 m buffer (D-007). It costs ×3.9 rows (or ×2 spans) and ×3 cover time.
- With the `ceil` rule z21 looks better (20.5 → 7.9 m), but only because `ceil` is the larger error; fix the rule instead.

## Recommendation (G1a, G1b)

**G1(a): quadkey, not H3.** On every axis that matters to FootNoted, the quadkey grid wins or ties:

1. **Compute.** Covering the 1,000 km × 500 m drive takes **~80 ms** at z20, against **1.7–2.5 s** for H3 r11 and **12–14 s** for r12 (raw FFI). The quadkey cover is pure Dart and needs no native code.
2. **Rendering.** Tile-aligned rectangles: about **88 vertices per z10 tile** from the z16 rollup, built in milliseconds. H3 needs a dissolve (2–468 s), or hundreds of thousands of hexagons, plus polygon clipping at tile edges. Its parent hierarchy is not geometrically exact, which breaks the "fog correct at every zoom" exit criterion 10 unless every coarse view is drawn from the leaf cells.
3. **Storage.** H3 has fewer cells at r11 (0.33× z20) but **no run-length span form**: 16 k spans at z20 against 485 k cells (or 71 k compacted) for H3 r11.
4. **Dependencies.** H3 adds a one-maintainer native plugin: CMake on Android, a prebuilt binary and CocoaPods-only on iOS, a discontinued transitive package, and BigInt overhead that would push us to write our own FFI.
5. **Where H3 is better.** Equal-area cells, so "km² explored" is simply count × area, and the most accurate edges (r12: ~2 m). Neither outweighs the above. For quadkey, area statistics need a per-row cos² weight, which is cheap because the row determines the latitude.

**G1(b): z20, not z21, provided F01.6 uses the exact radius rule** (the per-row radius is the real (b/w), not ⌈b/w⌉).

- At z20 with the exact rule, the measured edge error is 8–12 m per side, equal to z21 with the handoff's `ceil` rule.
- z21 would cost **×3.9 per-cell rows or ×2 spans** and ×3 reveal time, for ≤ 1.3–5 m of edge accuracy. That is below GPS noise, and invisible once the fog is blocky at z16–z18 (32–128 dp per cell at z20).
- If G1 keeps `ceil`, z20 overreaches by about 20 m on 100 m buffers. Fix the rule rather than raise the level.

## Out-of-contract findings

1. **The F01.6 / handoff dilation rule** ("radius in cells = ceil(buffer ÷ cell width)") systematically inflates coverage by about one cell. Changing it to the exact radius (or a distance-to-segment test) is a G1/F01 decision and a handoff text change. Raised, not changed (orchestrator- and S01-31-owned).
2. **The handoff's storage estimate** ("roughly 700,000 z20 cells" for 1,000 km × 500 m) is the equatorial value. At 45°N it is 1.46 M. For G1(c) and S01-11.
3. **Environment side effects outside the repo.** The first `flutter build apk` made Gradle auto-install **Android SDK Platform 35** and **CMake 3.22.1** into the shared `D:\software\android-sdk`. Any future H3 adoption makes CMake a build prerequisite, including the CI Android job.
4. **Shared emulator.**
   - At the start of run 1 I ran `adb logcat -c` once, which clears the **shared** log buffer and may have cost a sibling session log lines. Later runs used `logcat -T 1` instead.
   - Run 3 was frozen when `render_bench` took the foreground. I force-stopped only my own app (`com.example.footnoted.spike.h3_eval`). I uninstalled nothing.
